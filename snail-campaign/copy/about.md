# About

---

## Our mission

Snail Welfare Alliance exists to end the needless killing of sea snails. Specifically: the lethal extraction of Tyrian purple from murex snails, for a dye whose identical synthetic substitute has been available since 1904.

That's it. One unnecessary practice. One specific molecule. The clearest, most immediately winnable front we could find.

SWA is an open coalition — researchers, designers, donors, and advocates across disciplines and backgrounds. Not a top-down charity asking for sympathy, but a coordinated effort to make one small, specific, obviously winnable argument land.

---

## Why snails

Because nobody is watching them.

Invertebrate welfare is the most neglected corner of an already under-resourced field. Billions of snails are killed each year for food, often by boiling alive, with no welfare standards and essentially no public awareness. Shrimp numbers run into the trillions. The science on invertebrate nociception is advancing faster than the policy, the culture, or the conversation.

We're not claiming snails definitely suffer the way mammals do. We don't know that. What we know is: they have the biology for it, their relatives are now legally recognized as sentient in several jurisdictions, and the question is worth taking seriously.

The Tyrian purple case is our opening front because it combines everything we look for: an unnecessary practice, a luxury market, a provably identical alternative, and a scale small enough that a focused campaign can actually move the needle.

---

## Why now

In 2023, Conagen announced commercial fermentation-based Tyrian purple — the identical molecule, made by microbes, no animals. The last technical argument for lethal extraction is gone. What remains is inertia, aesthetics, and the idea that "natural is better." We exist to challenge that idea, specifically in this context, with the facts it cannot survive.

---

## Our scope

Snails broadly. Tyrian purple is our opening front, not our limit. We're watching developments in food-snail welfare, research-use standards, and invertebrate sentience science. The Shrimp Welfare Project has shown that focused, evidence-based advocacy can change farming practices for invertebrates once considered too alien to matter. We think snails are next.

---

## Our honesty policy

We do not overclaim. We state what the science shows and what it doesn't. We present the precautionary argument openly as a precautionary argument — not as proven fact. We name producers and practices that are on public record; we do not attack struggling artisans or religious communities. We target the demand: collectors, institutions, and buyers who specifically seek out lethal natural Tyrian purple when a perfect substitute exists.

If we get something wrong, we correct it. The credibility of this campaign is worth more than any single claim.

---

## What we're not

We are not a general animal rights organization. We are not anti-fishing. We are not targeting the Mixtec dyers of Oaxaca, who have practiced a non-lethal tradition for 3,000 years and are, in fact, our best evidence that this argument is right.

We don't ask anyone to give anything up. We ask them to make one extremely small, completely cost-free substitution: the same molecule, from a flask.
